Broadband and mobile coverage are two of the few things on a moving checklist that genuinely can't be judged from the area's general reputation - both depend on the exact building, not just the postcode district.

## Check the actual address, not the general area

Broadband speed depends on the specific infrastructure serving your building - which exchange or cabinet it's connected to, whether full-fibre has reached that street yet, or whether it's still relying on the older copper network for the final stretch. Two flats in the same block can occasionally get different results depending on internal wiring, but checking speeds available at the exact address, not just "the area," is the reliable way to know what you'll actually get.

## Order earlier than feels necessary

Some installations, particularly full-fibre where it's newly available, can take several weeks rather than the standard few days - if you have a fixed move-in date and want service from day one, it's worth ordering as soon as you have the new address confirmed, not waiting until you've moved in. A short gap without home broadband is a minor inconvenience; discovering the wait is six weeks after you've already moved is a bigger one.

## Mobile signal is separately variable

Good broadband at an address says nothing about mobile signal strength, which depends on network and building construction as much as location - thick walls, certain building materials, and even which floor you're on can noticeably affect indoor signal for one network while barely touching another. If you're tied to a long mobile contract, checking indoor coverage for your specific network at the new address, not just "does this area have signal," avoids an unpleasant surprise on a contract you can't easily leave early.

## What to actually do

Use an address-level (not just postcode-level) checker for both broadband and mobile before choosing a provider or signing anything, and if you're moving between London boroughs and want a general sense of how connectivity compares as one factor among several, PostcodeHub's [Compare areas](/compare/) tool shows it alongside crime, transport and other numbers for a shortlist of districts.
