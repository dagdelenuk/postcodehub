London has 33 boroughs plus the City of London, and most people considering a move are realistically choosing between two or three of them, or a handful of postcode districts within one. The hard part usually isn't a lack of information - it's that the information arrives unevenly, one fact at a time from different places, which makes it easy to over-weight whatever you happened to read most recently.

## Pick the factors before you start looking

It helps to decide upfront what actually matters for your situation, rather than discovering it halfway through research. For most people moving within London, the recurring ones are:

- **Housing cost** - both to buy and to rent, since the two don't always move together
- **Commute** - to the actual place you'd be travelling to, not to "central London" in general
- **Safety** - recorded crime, ideally set against the borough's own recent trend, not just a single total
- **Schools** - if relevant, both how many options exist nearby and their current Ofsted picture
- **Environment** - green space access and road noise, which shape daily life more than people expect until they've lived somewhere noisy or somewhere green

## Compare like for like, not headline to headline

A borough-wide average can hide real variation - a borough with a high average sale price can still have individual postcode districts that are comparatively affordable, and vice versa. It's worth comparing at whichever level actually matches your decision: borough-to-borough if you're choosing broadly, or specific postcode districts against each other if you've already narrowed it down. PostcodeHub's [Compare areas](/compare/) tool works at both levels - boroughs or individual districts - side by side on the same page.

## Use rankings to sanity-check, not to decide

A "quietest boroughs" or "best broadband" ranking is a useful sense-check - does the area you're leaning towards actually perform reasonably on the things you care about, or is it an outlier in a way you hadn't noticed? PostcodeHub's [Rankings](/london/rankings/) cover several of these dimensions across every borough and district. They're a good way to catch a blind spot, not a substitute for looking at the specific area itself.

## Bring it together on one borough's own page

Once you've got a shortlist, each borough's own [Statistics page](/london/) brings housing, demographics, crime, connectivity and environment together in one place, each section set against the London average so you can see at a glance whether a figure is genuinely unusual or fairly typical. That's usually a better final check than any single number in isolation - the goal isn't to find a borough with no downsides, it's to understand clearly which trade-offs you're actually making.
