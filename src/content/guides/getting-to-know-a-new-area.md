The big decisions about where to live are usually made well before moving day - price, commute, schools. Actually settling in comes down to a handful of smaller, practical things that are easy to leave until you stumble across them by accident.

## The basics worth locating early

The nearest park, library, supermarket and a couple of places to eat are worth finding in your first week or two rather than discovering gradually - not because any single one is urgent, but because knowing where they are removes a small daily friction that otherwise lingers for months. Libraries in particular are often underused simply because people don't realise how close the nearest one is or what it offers beyond books (many run free events, have quiet workspace, and offer printing).

## Who represents you locally

Finding your local councillors takes a couple of minutes on your council's website, and it's worth doing even if you don't expect to need them soon - they're the direct contact point for a local issue that a general council report form doesn't resolve (see "reporting problems" for when that comes up), and knowing who they are before you need them saves time later.

## Community spaces and allotments

If gardening or growing your own food interests you, allotment waiting lists in London can be long, so applying early (even before you're sure you'll want one) is worth it purely because of the lead time, not because you need to decide immediately. Community centres and local Facebook or WhatsApp groups for a street or estate are also a genuinely useful, if less official, way to find out what's actually happening locally - a missed bin collection or a planned road closure often surfaces there before it's obvious anywhere else.

## Food hygiene ratings

If you're exploring places to eat nearby, hygiene ratings (from the Food Standards Agency) are public and worth a quick check for anywhere you're trying for the first time, particularly if it's somewhere new rather than an established chain.

## No need to rush this

Unlike Council Tax or GP registration, none of this has a real deadline - the value is spread out over the following months rather than concentrated in the first week. Still, doing a small amount of this early (the park, the library, the councillor) tends to make the area feel familiar faster than waiting for it to happen on its own.
