Ofsted's four-word ratings — Outstanding, Good, Requires improvement, Inadequate — are probably the single most-quoted fact about any English school. They're genuinely useful, but they answer a narrower question than most people assume.

## What the rating is actually assessing

An Ofsted inspection judges a school against a defined framework: quality of education, behaviour and attitudes, personal development, and leadership and management, alongside safeguarding. It is a snapshot of how the school is run and taught, checked against a common standard - not a measure of exam results on its own, and not a ranking against other schools nearby.

## The date matters more than people think

A rating is only as current as its inspection date. Ofsted moved to a framework in late 2024 where some schools no longer receive a single combined headline grade at all, only an "outcome" per area — which means two schools both showing "Good" on paper can have been assessed under genuinely different systems, at very different points in time. A "Good" from a routine inspection five years ago and one from three months ago carry different weight. It's worth checking the inspection date alongside the grade, not just the grade on its own.

## Independent schools sit outside this system

A meaningful share of independent schools have no current Ofsted grade at all - they're inspected by different bodies (such as the Independent Schools Inspectorate), or simply haven't had a recent full inspection logged in the same dataset. On PostcodeHub's [Schools pages](/london/), a school shown with no rating usually falls into this category rather than having failed an inspection - the "At a glance" summary at the top of each borough's Schools page states the actual proportion with a current rating, so you can see how much of the picture Ofsted is even covering in that area.

## What it doesn't tell you

- Whether a specific child will do well there - that depends on fit, not just inspection outcomes
- Anything about exam or SATs results directly - Ofsted's framework doesn't publish those as part of the headline grade
- Whether the school suits a particular need - special educational needs support, a specific curriculum emphasis, or extracurricular strengths aren't captured in a single word

## A sensible way to use it

Treat a current, recent Outstanding or Good rating as a reasonable baseline - a signal that a school is being run competently - rather than a full picture. For anything that matters to a specific decision (SEN provision, a particular subject strength, the actual atmosphere of the place), the inspection report itself, an open day, and speaking to current parents will tell you far more than the headline word does.
