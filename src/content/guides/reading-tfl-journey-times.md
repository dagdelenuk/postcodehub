Two areas can both show "38 minutes to Bank" and still be genuinely different journeys to live with every day. The headline time is a fair starting point, but a few other details tend to matter more once you're actually doing it five days a week.

## Direct versus changes

A single number doesn't say whether it's one train the whole way or two changes with a platform walk in between. A 35-minute journey with one change is usually a more pleasant daily habit than a 30-minute one with two, especially with luggage, a pushchair, or during a delay - and delays compound faster the more changes are involved, since one late connection can cascade into a much longer wait for the next.

## Off-peak isn't the same as your actual departure time

Journey planners can quote a fast, quiet, off-peak route that simply doesn't reflect what a 8am departure looks like. Peak-time frequency, crowding on that specific line, and whether your station is a starting point (where you can usually get a seat) or partway along a route (where you often can't) all shape the real experience more than the timetabled minutes do.

## The whole journey, not just the train

The time from home to the platform, and from the final station to the office, is real commuting time too - it just doesn't always get top billing in a headline figure. A district a few minutes further from central London by train but a two-minute walk to the station can beat one that's nominally faster but starts with a 15-minute bus.
