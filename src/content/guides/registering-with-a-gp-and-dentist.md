Registering with a GP and a dentist is one of the cheapest, fastest things on a moving checklist to actually do - which is exactly why it's the one people put off. The practical reality of each is different enough that it's worth treating them separately.

## GP registration is more informal than people expect

You don't legally need proof of address or ID to register with a GP practice in England - a practice can ask for it, but can't refuse you for not having it. Most practices now let you register online in a few minutes through the NHS website or the practice's own site, and same-day acceptance is common if you're within their catchment area. The one thing worth checking first is whether a practice is actually accepting new patients at all - some in high-demand areas close their list temporarily, so it's worth having two or three nearby options in mind rather than assuming the closest one will take you.

## What happens to your medical record

Once you register with a new practice, your medical record is normally requested and transferred automatically in the background - you don't need to chase your old practice yourself. This can take anywhere from a few days to a few weeks, so if you're on repeat prescriptions, order enough beforehand to cover the gap, or ask your old practice for an emergency supply if you're moving with only days of medication left.

## Registering children

Children can be registered on the same form as an adult in most cases, and it's worth doing at the same time as the rest of the household rather than waiting - particularly for a baby or toddler due for routine vaccinations, since those are scheduled by age and a gap in cover can mean falling behind the immunisation schedule.

## Dentists are the real bottleneck

NHS dental capacity is tighter than GP capacity in most of the country, and "no NHS dentists accepting new patients nearby" is a genuinely common situation, not a sign you're doing something wrong. If nothing nearby has availability, the NHS's own find-a-dentist search lets you widen the radius, and it's worth checking back periodically since availability changes. A private dentist is usually a faster fallback if you need urgent treatment before an NHS slot opens up, though obviously not free.

## A sensible order to do this in

Register with a GP practice in your first week if you can, even before you've fully settled in - it costs nothing and there's no reason to wait. Start looking for a dentist at the same time rather than after, given the longer typical wait, and don't leave repeat prescriptions until you're down to your last few days' supply.
