Council Tax is one of the few moving tasks with a real deadline attached: liability starts the day you move in, not the day you get around to registering. Nobody chases you for this immediately, but the sooner it's sorted, the less awkward the backdated bill.

## Tell both councils, not just the new one

If you're moving within England, you generally need to tell your outgoing council that you've left as well as telling your new council that you've arrived - the two aren't automatically linked, even if you're moving between two London boroughs. Most councils now handle this through an online form rather than a phone call, and it typically only takes a few minutes if you have your move-in date and new address to hand.

## Set up how you'll pay early

Direct Debit is usually the easiest way to pay, and setting it up as soon as you've registered avoids a paper bill turning up for an amount you weren't expecting. Most councils let you choose which day of the month it comes out and whether you pay over 10 or 12 months - spreading it over 12 lowers the individual payment if a lower fixed amount matters more to you than paying it off two months early.

## Check what discounts and exemptions apply

The most common is the 25% single-person discount, which applies if you're the only adult living in the property - it's not automatic, so you need to apply for it even if you know you qualify. Full-time students can be exempt entirely, and there are separate discounts and reductions for some disabled residents, people on low incomes (Council Tax Reduction, means-tested and worth checking even if you weren't eligible for anything similar before), and properties that are empty or undergoing major repair. None of these get applied retroactively without you asking, so it's worth checking the full list on your council's website rather than assuming you don't qualify for anything.

## What your band actually costs

Your liability is set by your property's Council Tax band and your council's rate for that band that year - not by how many people live there beyond the single-person discount. If you want to understand what determines the band itself and how to compare the real cost across different boroughs, that's covered separately in [what Council Tax bands mean for your budget](/guides/council-tax-bands-explained/).

## If you disagree with your band

You can challenge a property's Council Tax band if you think it's wrong, but this is a separate process from registering to pay - don't hold up registering while you look into a challenge, since you're still liable for the current band in the meantime and can get a refund later if a challenge succeeds.
