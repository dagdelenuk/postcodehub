Updating your address is one of those tasks that's individually trivial but collectively easy to half-finish - a handful of organisations get told straight away, and a few important ones get forgotten for months. A rough order of operations helps.

## Do these first

Your driving licence address can be updated free online through GOV.UK, and it's worth doing promptly since driving with an out-of-date licence address can technically be an offence, even though it's rarely enforced on its own. Tell HMRC too - your address affects your tax code, Child Benefit, and anything to do with Self Assessment, and letters about all three go to whatever address they hold.

## Financial and contractual

Your bank, credit card providers, and any insurers (car, home, life) need your correct address both for correspondence and because it can genuinely affect your risk profile and premiums - home and car insurance in particular are priced partly by postcode, so an out-of-date address on a policy can complicate a claim later. Your employer needs it too, both for payroll correspondence and for anything HR sends by post.

## The electoral roll

Being on the electoral register at your current address isn't just about voting - lenders and phone providers use it as part of standard credit checks, so an out-of-date entry can quietly affect things like a mortgage application or a new phone contract months later, well after the move itself. It's a genuinely fast form to update.

## TV Licence

If you have one, it's tied to an address, not a person, so it needs updating (or a new one taking out) as soon as you move in - technically you need a valid licence from the day you start watching or streaming live TV at the new address, not from whenever you get round to updating the old one.

## Royal Mail redirection

Setting up mail redirection is a paid service, but it's worth it for at least the first few months after a move, since it catches anything you've missed telling directly - it typically takes a few working days to start once set up, so it's worth arranging it before you move rather than after post has already started bouncing back to your old address. It can be set up for a few months or up to a year, and covers named individuals at the address, not just "the household."

## A rough priority order

Driving licence and HMRC first (both free, both quick, both have minor consequences for delay), electoral roll and Royal Mail redirection next (the electoral roll matters more than people expect, and redirection needs lead time to kick in), then bank, insurers and employer as you go.
